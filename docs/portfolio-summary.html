Energy monitoring for a 3D printer park, built in Microsoft Fabric

Portfolio project by Vincent Antoine · github.com/v-nc/fabric-am-energy · October 2026
Personal portfolio project on simulated data. It rebuilds, on Microsoft Fabric, an energy-monitoring system I built in 2019 for an internal park of 3D printers (OPC UA aggregation, InfluxDB, Grafana, alerts on over-long heat-ups). No real machine, customer or company data is used. DP-700 (Fabric Data Engineer Associate) is in preparation.

What I built

  • Edge: OPC UA simulator and a TypeScript gateway with a SQLite store-and-forward buffer, per-meter sequence numbers and simulated network outages (late, out-of-order, duplicate events).
  • Ingestion: Eventstream custom endpoint over the Event Hubs protocol; 12 months of history (5.2M rows, two schema versions) loaded in full and incrementally with a watermark.
  • Silver: Spark Structured Streaming (availableNow), quality rules with a quarantine table, idempotent insert-only MERGE.
  • Gold: star schema with energy from kWh counters, a time-of-use tariff in local time, build jobs, heat-ups and an SCD type 2 machine dimension.
  • BI: Direct Lake semantic model with DAX measures, a coverage grid for completeness, five report pages stored as PBIR JSON and deployed from Git.
  • Operations: daily pipeline with retries, table maintenance (OPTIMIZE, V-Order, VACUUM) and an explicit model reframe; paid F4 capacity scripted and paused when idle (about €8 so far).

Decisions worth discussing

  • Why the gateway sends live data first after an outage, and how silver copes with late data.
  • Why deduplicate on (meter, measurement time) rather than on the sequence number.
  • Energy from counter deltas, and plausibility rules that turned 217,000 kWh of phantom energy into 10 flagged discontinuities.
  • Why an SCD2 dimension needs its surrogate key in every relationship, and how that breaks row-level security if you get it wrong.
  • Direct Lake framing: why the model is reframed once at the end of the pipeline.
  • Transformations as a tested Python package, shipped to Fabric as a wheel in an Environment.
